# Examples

Illustrative examples, with made-up data to show the shape. Don't copy their names or figures into the user's work.

## Email: from chronological order to pyramid

**Before**
> Subject: Yesterday's meeting
> Hi Anna. Yesterday we met with the team to review the campaign's progress. We went over the metrics for the first three weeks, talked with the sales team and also looked at ad spend. Sales mentioned that leads come in with very little information. Cost per lead is up 30% versus plan. The current form only asks for name and email. We think some adjustments are needed. Can we talk about it?

**Diagnosis:** the main idea doesn't appear; the order is the meeting's order; "some adjustments" is an empty synthesis; there's no request and no deadline.

**Pyramid**
```
Anna's question: what do you need from me?
Main idea: Approve changing the form this week.
  1. Cost per lead is 30% over plan.
  2. Sales gets leads with no data to qualify them.
  (comparative order: the cost problem first)
```

**After**
> Subject: Can you approve changing the campaign form this week?
> Hi Anna. I'm asking you to approve a change to the campaign form this week, to tackle two problems we saw yesterday:
> 1. Cost per lead is 30% above plan.
> 2. Sales gets leads with only name and email, with no data to qualify them.
> The proposal is to add company and job title. If you confirm today, I'll coordinate the change.

Nothing was added. What changed was the order and the explicit request. It doesn't claim the change will lower the cost, because the draft doesn't support that.

## Review: reply when the user only shared their text

> "Your recommendation only shows up in the last paragraph, after recounting the whole meeting. Want me to reorder it with the conclusion first?"

Two sentences. No rewritten version.

## Mechanism versus reason

A poorly built first level, for "why should we buy this software?":
1. It integrates with our ERP.
2. It has configurable dashboards.
3. It exports to Excel.

All three read well as "the system can...": they're mechanisms. Corrected:
1. We'll see margin per client every week, not at quarter close. (It integrates with the ERP)
2. Each manager will track their own metrics without asking finance for them. (Configurable dashboards)

"Exports to Excel" doesn't support any reason on its own: it goes in as a detail or comes out.

## Academic: a paper's introduction as SCQ

```
S (context):       Hybrid work has become the norm in many service companies.
C (gap):           There are almost no studies on its effect on the productivity of Latin American SMEs.
Q (question):      How does an SME's productivity change when it moves to hybrid work?
A (contribution):  This study measures [missing: sample] and finds [missing: main finding].
```

The rest of the paper follows IMRaD; the pyramid is applied within the discussion, where each paragraph opens with its claim.
